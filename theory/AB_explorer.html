View

1  Single-period return

R(r) = r + period dividend + f·ỹ − f·(r − c)+. q is an annualized pricing input only; this toy view uses dq = exp(qτ) − 1 as the period-dividend proxy. A is capped when fA = 1; B keeps slope 1 − fB above cB.

A — fA @ cA B — fB @ cB buy & hold

2  D(r) = RA − RB

For a valid equal-premium comparison, premium and common dividend terms cancel exactly → a zero-cost ratio call spread. Peak fB(cA−cB) at r = cA, second zero at r**.

3  Sweep cA — the (f, c) trade-off

Dpeak(c) = c·fB(c) behaves like a price×quantity curve: at short tenor it has a real interior maximum (marked, where widening the strike further starts losing more coverage fB than the wider gap gains); at long tenor that maximum moves past 25% OTM, so within this window the curve is monotone and concave, not flat. r** always keeps rising. Everything else held at the current slider values.

4  Sweep tenor — the term structure

Same Dpeak / r** pair, now against tenor at the current caps — this is the axis to drag to see how the structure evolves over months, instead of reading it off chart 4 at a fixed tenor. Dpeak rises and flattens (more coverage fB is needed at longer tenor to match the same premium, so A's edge grows but decelerates); r** rises almost linearly — a longer-dated A needs a much bigger rally before B catches up.

5  Term grid — strike × tenor

Every cell prices the full-overwrite leg at that cap and tenor on the current smile (σ_ATM, wing) and carry inputs; fB / r** / Dpeak match it against the same-tenor anchor at cB. “—” = infeasible: cap below cB, or wing outside its feasible domain at that strike (vol positivity / vertical-spread no-arbitrage).
